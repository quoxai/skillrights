## Part 1: answers to the 12 brief questions

1. **Copyright floor.** A skill's procedures and decision rules are methods, excluded from copyright (17 U.S.C. 102(b); UK idea/expression doctrine). What is protectable is the concrete expression: the prose, the ordering and selection, the examples, any code. Short highly-functional texts sit near the merger doctrine (where an idea is expressible in few ways, expression may be unprotected). Consequence: the licences must operate even where copyright is thin, which means (a) the grant/reservation language should be framed "to the extent rights subsist", and (b) contract and marketplace terms carry real weight. Section 7's fallback is useful but currently passive; see defect D8.
2. **Art 4(3) reservation.** The wording is directionally right but incomplete. Art 4(3) requires reservation "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means" by the rightsholder. Recital 18 contemplates metadata and website terms. A LICENSE file plus a frontmatter identifier plausibly qualifies, but the strongest posture is redundancy: licence text (present), one-line header (present), plus repo/site-level TDMRep and robots signals (CLI emits, licence should reference). Add a sentence that the reservation stands regardless of which copy or surface a miner encountered. The purpose-scoping (reserving TDM only for training purposes, leaving search/indexing free) is deliberate and should survive review; it makes the reservation narrower and more defensible, not less.
3. **Train definition and the RAG boundary.** The definition is purpose-scoped ("assembled for any of those purposes"), which does the work, but the execution/retrieval carve-out lives only in SPEC.md. A boundary this load-bearing belongs in the licence text itself. See D3. Otherwise the broad single definition is sound; splitting fine-tuning from distillation would create unobservable distinctions.
4. **Privity and fair use.** Confirmed as the brief states it. Against a non-party whose use is fair use, the licence yields no copyright claim; its value there is (a) a dated, unambiguous record of non-consent should US law evolve (statutory or common-law), (b) contract where any relationship exists (marketplace, API terms, employment), (c) the EU route via Art 53(1)(c) AI Act, which binds GPAI providers placing models on the EU market regardless of training location. Public claims are currently calibrated correctly in SPEC.md; keep them.
5. **Termination.** Automatic termination with downstream survival is standard and fine. Defect: no cure period, which punishes inadvertent redistribution breaches as harshly as training. Training is irreversible so no cure there, but attribution/notice breaches deserve a 30-day cure (GPLv3 precedent). See D5.
6. **Authorised Recipient (Reserved).** Two defects: an "AI system" cannot be a licensee (not a legal person), and "within their own organisation or for their own use" is ambiguous for affiliates and contractors. See D6, D7.
7. **Warranty/liability.** "To the maximum extent permitted by law" is the correct savings formula for UK unfair-terms and consumer exposure. Adequate for v1.0. Individual licensors cannot exclude liability for fraud or (UK) death/personal injury by negligence; the savings clause handles this implicitly. Acceptable.
8. **Governing law.** Stay silent, following MIT/CC practice. Specifying England and Wales would narrow international adoption and buys little for a standard whose enforcement is mostly contractual-by-context (marketplace ToS can and should specify law).
9. **Trademark.** Separate workstream; the licences should meanwhile state they grant no trademark rights (see D9). Clearance search remains a to-do that AI review cannot substitute for; flag as the one unavoidable piece of professional work before heavy brand investment.
10. **Marketplace clause.** Draft (for QuoxSkills ToS): "Each skill available through the Service is provided under the rights declaration stated on its listing. By downloading or executing a skill you agree to be bound by that declaration as a contract between you and the skill's licensor, in addition to these Terms. Where the declaration reserves training rights, you agree not to use the skill, in whole or in part, for the training, fine-tuning, distillation or synthetic-training-data derivation of any machine-learning model." This is where enforcement is strongest; it binds parties regardless of the copyright floor.
11. **SPDX.** Submission requires the text be final and immutable; the steward commitment is procedural, not legal risk. No blocker. Submit after 1.0 cut.
12. **Public claims.** SPEC.md's "What this can and cannot do" is accurate against findings 1 to 4. One understatement: it does not mention that marketplace/contractual enforcement is fully available today; one line would strengthen honesty in the favourable direction (underselling is also an honesty failure).

## Part 2: drafting defects and exact fixes

- **D1 (MAJOR, Open + Reserved).** Cross-licence incorporation by reference ("meanings given in the SkillRights NoTrain Licence 1.0") breaks when a LICENSE file travels alone, and references a not-yet-existing final text. Fix: make each licence self-contained; repeat the four definitions verbatim in each text.
- **D2 (MAJOR, all).** No definition of "Licensor" or "you". Fix, add to section 1: "'Licensor' means the person or entity offering the Work under this licence. 'You' means the individual or entity exercising rights under it."
- **D3 (MAJOR, NoTrain + Reserved).** RAG boundary absent from licence text. Fix, append to the Train definition: "For clarity, retrieval, indexing or embedding of the Work solely to enable its execution at inference time is Execution, not Training."
- **D4 (MINOR, all).** Rights-subsistence framing. Fix, add to section 1: "'Work' includes its elements to the extent rights subsist in them; nothing in this licence asserts rights over ideas, methods or processes as such."
- **D5 (MAJOR, NoTrain + Reserved).** No cure period. Fix, replace termination section: "Your rights terminate automatically if you Train on the Work. For any other material breach, your rights terminate if the breach is not cured within 30 days of you becoming aware of it. Rights of parties who received the Work from you in compliance with this licence survive."
- **D6 (MAJOR, Reserved).** AI systems as licensees. Fix: "'Authorised Recipient' means a person or organisation that has received the Work directly from the Licensor, or with the Licensor's express permission, under this licence. Rights granted to an Authorised Recipient extend to AI or software systems acting on its behalf."
- **D7 (MINOR, Reserved).** Organisational scope. Fix, in the grant: "within its own organisation (including affiliates and contractors acting for it) or for its own use".
- **D8 (MINOR, NoTrain + Reserved).** Passive fallback clause. Strengthen modestly without overclaiming browsewrap: "Where this licence cannot bind a party as a matter of copyright law, it stands as a stated condition of access, a term of any contract under which the Work is obtained, and a documented, dated reservation of consent."
- **D9 (MINOR, all).** Trademark. Add to miscellaneous: "This licence grants no rights in any trade mark or name, including 'SkillRights'."
- **D10 (MINOR, NoTrain).** Art 4(3) redundancy sentence. Add to section 3: "This reservation applies to every copy of the Work however encountered, whether or not a given copy carries the licence header."
- **D11 (MINOR, Open).** "Training does not require attribution" is fine, but Open should also state (symmetry, and to prevent licence-stripping): "Redistribution of modified versions must not remove or misstate this licence's identifier."
- **D12 (MINOR, all).** Bottom-of-file identifier/URL blocks reference -draft URLs; at 1.0 cut, URLs must point to the immutable /1.0 paths and the site must serve both.
