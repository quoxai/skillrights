SkillRights

An open licence family for AI agent skills: execution permitted, training rights reserved, in one line.

Agent skill files (for example Anthropic's SKILL.md format) already define an optional license frontmatter field. SkillRights uses it, unchanged:

SKILL.md frontmatter
---
name: cluster-doctor
description: Diagnose failing clusters the way a 15-year SRE does.
license: LicenseRef-SkillRights-NoTrain-1.0
---

For human readers, crawlers and corpus filters, add one line near the top of the file body or README:

README / file-body line
SkillRights-NoTrain-1.0: AI execution permitted. Model training rights reserved. https://skillrights.org/notrain/1.0

That is the whole integration. About 20 tokens, loaded only when the skill is invoked. No AI model needs to read legal prose for the declaration to work: installers, marketplaces, crawlers and courts are the audience.

The problem#

A skill is executable expertise: procedures, judgement and methodology written down so a machine can act on them. Publishing one so agents can use it should not silently grant permission to absorb it into a model. Most licences in use today were never written with that distinction in mind, so authors either say nothing (and get trained on by default) or reach for a licence that blocks the AI use they actually wanted.

SkillRights separates four permissions that current licences blur together: Execute (an AI agent or a human may read and use the skill to perform work), Train (the skill's content may be used to train, fine-tune, distil, or derive training data for a model), Redistribute (the skill may be copied, republished, or included in other collections), and Commercial (the skill may be executed in commercial settings).

The three licences#

SkillRights-Open-1.0

  • Execute yes
  • Train yes
  • Redistribute yes (attribution)
  • Commercial yes

SkillRights-NoTrain-1.0

  • Execute yes
  • Train reserved
  • Redistribute yes (attribution)
  • Commercial yes

SkillRights-Reserved-1.0

  • Execute authorised recipients only
  • Train reserved
  • Redistribute reserved
  • Commercial by agreement

Named variants, not checkboxes: three reviewed texts beat an unreviewable matrix. Open exists because a rights standard that cannot express "take it, learn from it, improve everything" is an anti-AI posture, and this is not one. Boundary rule for retrieval: using a skill in a retrieval or RAG pipeline at inference time is execution. Building an embedding or corpus for model improvement is training.

How it works#

  1. Add one line. Put the licence identifier in your SKILL.md frontmatter, or a one-line header near the top of the file body or README.
  2. Publish. Per SPDX LicenseRef- convention, include the licence text in a LICENSES/ directory (the skillrights CLI does this for you).
  3. Hosts and crawlers read it. No AI model needs to read legal prose for the declaration to work: installers, marketplaces, crawlers and courts are the audience.

What this can and cannot do#

Honesty is a design requirement of this specification, so:

What it does

  • In the EU, Article 4(3) of the DSM Directive allows relevant rightholders to reserve rights against the Article 4 text-and-data-mining exception; for publicly available online content the reservation should be expressed by appropriate machine-readable means, and the EU AI Act (Art 53(1)(c), in force since August 2025) requires providers of general-purpose AI models placed on the EU market to have a copyright-compliance policy that identifies and complies with such reservations. SkillRights provides a stated reservation and, where implemented, companion technical signals.
  • In the US, a SkillRights text is a copyright licence for protected expression and may be a contract where a user affirmatively accepts it. It is dated evidence of a stated reservation, and its evidential weight grows with provenance (signing, timestamping, publication).
  • Within a marketplace or other contracting relationship, the declared terms can be enforceable where they are clearly presented, affirmatively accepted, supported by a valid contract, and consistent with mandatory law.

What it does not guarantee

  • Whether a particular signal qualifies, and whether the content and use fall within the relevant law, depend on the facts and applicable national law. The Article 3 scientific-research exception is not subject to an Article 4(3) opt-out. Which exact formats qualify is being decided by the European Commission now.
  • In the UK, the DSM Directive does not itself apply. Separate UK copyright exceptions, including the non-commercial text-and-data-analysis exception, may apply.
  • Bartz v. Anthropic (2025) found training on lawfully acquired books to be fair use, and no court has yet ruled on whether a machine-readable reservation changes that analysis. A declaration does not by itself bind a non-assenting person, eliminate fair use, or prevent scraping.
  • They are not automatically or universally enforceable, but this is where enforcement is strongest today.
